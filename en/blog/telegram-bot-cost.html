How Much Does a Telegram Bot Cost in 2026: Full Price Breakdown

📅 July 2026 ⏱ 9 minutes ✍️ Overboard Studio

Telegram bot prices vary wildly — from $60 to $3,600. If you're ordering a bot for the first time, it's hard to make sense of it. This article gives you an honest breakdown: what types of bots exist, what actually drives the price, and where the line is between "cheap and risky" and "worth every dollar."

Quick answer: a simple lead collection bot starts at $120, a booking bot with reminders starts at $300, an AI bot with integrations starts at $600. Timeline: 3 to 20 days. A builder is cheaper upfront but costs more over 2 years.

Types of Telegram Bots and Their Prices

Before talking about price — it's important to understand that "a bot" isn't a single product. It's a spectrum of solutions with fundamentally different complexity. Here are the main types with real market prices:

1. Informational bot

The simplest type. Answers questions via buttons: schedule, address, pricing, FAQ. No logic — just pre-written responses. Great for small businesses that want to free up staff from repetitive questions. From $60–$180.

2. Lead collection bot

Collects name, phone number, and question from the user, then forwards to a manager via Telegram or email. Can integrate with Google Sheets or a CRM. The most popular type for small and mid-size businesses. From $120–$300.

3. Appointment booking bot

Lets clients choose a service, date, and time on their own. Sends reminders, handles cancellations and reschedules. Perfect for beauty salons, clinics, gyms, and tutors. From $300–$720.

4. E-commerce bot

Shows a catalog, takes orders, issues invoices, and notifies the manager. Can process payments directly inside Telegram via Stripe or other gateways — no redirect to a website needed. From $480–$1,200.

5. AI bot

Understands free-form text, responds like a live consultant, handles objections. Connected to ChatGPT or other language models. Ideal for online schools, complex services, and businesses with non-standard customer inquiries. From $600–$1,800.

6. Corporate internal bot

An internal tool for teams: task approvals, leave requests, CRM notifications, dashboards. Used by companies to automate internal workflows. From $720+.

Builder or Custom Python Development: Which to Choose

No-code builders (ManyChat, Chatfuel, Botmother) let you create a simple bot without coding. That's fine for testing an idea. But for ongoing use, they come with serious limitations.

Option 1
No-code builder
  • ✓ Quick to launch — within a day
  • ✓ Low upfront cost: $10–$60/mo
  • ✗ Monthly fee forever
  • ✗ Complex scenarios nearly impossible
  • ✗ No own database
  • ✗ Platform dependency
  • ✗ Costs more than custom dev over 2 years
Option 2 — better long-term
Custom Python (Aiogram 3)
  • ✓ Pay once — runs for years
  • ✓ Any logic and integrations
  • ✓ Own database (PostgreSQL/SQLite)
  • ✓ Full control over the code
  • ✓ Server from $2/mo, no dependency
  • ✗ Higher upfront cost
  • ✗ Needs a developer for changes

Bottom line: a builder makes sense if you need a bot for 1–2 months to test an idea. For permanent use, custom Python development pays for itself within 12–18 months and gives far more capability.

What Drives the Price

Two bots with similar descriptions can cost $120 and $960 — and both quotes can be fair. Here's what actually affects the price:

  • Number of scenarios and dialog branches — a bot with 3 buttons and one with 15 scenarios are fundamentally different scopes of work. Each branch requires separate logic, error handling, and edge cases.
  • Integrations — connecting to a CRM, Google Sheets, payment systems, email services — each integration is a separate task. One integration adds $36–$180 to the cost.
  • Database — if the bot needs to remember users, order history, or bookings, a database is required. This adds architectural complexity and development time.
  • Notifications and broadcasts — automated reminders, bulk messaging to your base — these are separate functional blocks.
  • AI and language models — connecting to GPT, prompt engineering, handling edge-case inputs — the most expensive component. Adds $240–$720.
  • Clarity of requirements — vague "I want a bot for my business" briefs make developers add risk buffers. A clear spec reduces the price by 15–30%.

Why a Bot Pays for Itself

Works 24/7. A client submits a lead at midnight — the bot catches it and notifies the manager. Without the bot, that lead is lost. Studies show 30–40% of online inquiries come outside business hours.

Reduces manager workload. Common questions — price, address, hours — the bot answers on its own. Your team only deals with warm, ready-to-buy leads.

Cuts no-shows. An automated reminder 24 hours before an appointment reduces no-shows by 30–50%. For a clinic or salon, that's hundreds of dollars recovered every month.

Builds your client database. Everyone who messages the bot is already in your base — ready for broadcasts, promotions, and repeat sales with zero ad spend.
